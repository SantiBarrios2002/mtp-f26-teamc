Suggested hardware for the transceiver

From Radio Team (Santiago, Andrian, Marcos)To Carlos, Michele, Bikash, GuillemVersion 0.2 · Fri 9 Oct 2026

The radio baseline is 868 MHz with the RFM69HCW. Under our split, the electronics team picks the micro and designs the board. This page is the radio team's suggestion, built around every hardware rule in the competition PDF, to save you time. It is not a decision. Please challenge it.

In short

  1. Micro module: Raspberry Pi Pico 2 (RP2350, not the Pico 2 W). It costs about $5, has 4 MB flash to hold the 1 MB file, 520 KB RAM and 3.3 V logic, and it can act as a USB host on any two pins or on its native USB port. It has no radio chip on board, so it is safe under the protocol ban.
  2. Radio module: Adafruit RFM69HCW breakout, 868/915 MHz (PID 3070), €13.65 excl. VAT. It mounts on 0.1" headers and has its own 3.3 V regulator and u.FL pads. We keep the same board from the bench to the final boxes, because modules shown at Quick Mode are final. The radio team's driver is already written and tested. (Changed in v0.2: v0.1 suggested the bare module.)
  3. Board: a custom 2-layer carrier PCB that the Pico 2 and the radio solder onto. It also carries the USB-A host port, the battery charger, the 5 V supply for the stick, ESD protection, the switch, button and LEDs, and a u.FL antenna connector. Before Quick Mode we use a perfboard prototype with the same modules.
  4. Power: one protected 18650 Li-ion cell, charged on board through a charge-only USB-C port. Our whole competition day uses roughly 10–20 % of it.
  5. Estimated cost: about €55–65 per device with a lab-made PCB, or about €40–50 with an external fab, against the €150 cap. The Adafruit breakout adds about €10 over a bare radio module.

Battery sizing

A sending round averages about 140 mA. Idle with the radio listening is about 50 mA. A full competition day of ~4 hours powered on needs about 250–400 mAh.

One 2500 mAh 18650 covers that about six times over. A smaller LiPo would also do, but pouch cells are more fragile in the drop test.

For the sustainability report

We can measure watts per round directly on this design. The parts are rechargeable and RoHS. The modules unsolder and can be reused, and the board shape lets you repair rather than replace.

Risks and how we cover them

RiskMitigation
PIO-USB host fails with some USB sticksTest 3–4 different sticks in stage 1. The RP2350's native USB can also be a host, so a second path exists on the same chip.
The foil-wrapped setup makes the USB port hard to reachPut the port on a side wall. Ask on Atenea whether the foil may be opened at the port during the 5-min setup.
Brown-out during radio TXBulk capacitance at the radio; check the battery at low charge (3.3 V) in stage 3.
Drop test breaks a joint or ejects the batteryNo sockets; standoffs; strapped cell; drop rev A on purpose in week 9.
The "attacks" interrupt the link mid-roundWatchdog and brown-out reset; firmware resumes from the last acknowledged line (protocols team).
