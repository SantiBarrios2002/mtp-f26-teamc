Radio brief: band, module and link budget

Team Santiago, Andrian, MarcosVersion 0.2 · Sun 4 Oct 2026Next session Fri 9 Oct (week 3)

New in v0.2: a working baseline (868 MHz + RFM69HCW), the duty-cycle limit that now drives our planning, the electronics handoff, a shared-PHY proposal for the other teams, and the test plan.

What the rules allow, what the numbers say, and what we need to decide first. Every figure carries a tag: rule from the competition rules PDF, verified against a primary source, assumption to be replaced by a measurement, and estimate.

Bottom line

  1. Only two bands allow a 2-minute continuous round: 869.40–869.65 MHz (500 mW e.r.p., ≤10 % duty cycle) and 2.4 GHz (10 mW e.i.r.p.). The 433 MHz antenna does not fit the box and the other 868 sub-bands fail on duty cycle. verified
  2. 868 MHz has range to spare but limited speed. Margin is +36 to +44 dB at 70 m and +19 to +21 dB at 260 m. The 250 kHz channel caps the air rate at about 100–150 kbps. That just covers our goal of 10,000 lines in 2 minutes (it needs ~67 kbps of useful throughput), so compression gives us the safety margin.
  3. 2.4 GHz (nRF24L01+) has speed to spare but thin margin: +6 to +9 dB at 70 m and −4.6 dB at 260 m with small whips. Directional 8 dBi patches bring 260 m back to +7 dB.
  4. Our band choice depends on the other teams. In NM every team must share one physical layer, and in MRM every team transmits at once. So the band talks with the other teams have to start this week, before we commit to a module. The module is frozen at Quick Mode, and changing it afterwards costs every bonus. rule
  5. Working baseline (v0.2): 868 MHz at 869.525 MHz with the RFM69HCW at +17 dBm and 100 kbps. 2.4 GHz nRF24 stays an add-on option for MRM. Santiago adopted this on 4 Oct so build work can start. Andrian and Marcos still need to sign off, by Fri 16 Oct at the latest.
  6. Duty cycle, not range or speed, is the binding limit. A 2-minute sending round is about 115 s of TX, and each box may transmit 360 s per hour. That is about 3 full rounds per hour per box, campus tests included. Repeated tests therefore go to 869.70–870.00 MHz (5 mW, no duty-cycle limit).

Duty-cycle budget per box

The model uses selective-repeat ARQ with one ACK per 16 frames.

  • Sender TX per 2-min round: 115.5 s
  • Receiver TX (ACKs) per round: 2.0 s
  • Budget in any one hour: 360 s (10 %)
  • Full sending rounds per hour: ≈3.1

The firmware refuses to transmit past 95 % of the hourly budget.

Firmware ready before the hardware

A portable C driver for the RFM69 is written and passes 154 host tests against a simulated radio. It needs only five board functions (SPI select and transfer, reset pin, delay, millisecond clock), so it fits whichever micro the electronics team picks.

It includes the duty-cycle guard and the software CRC for the NM link. Not yet run on a real module.

First antenna (QM)

A λ/4 wire (86 mm) bent as an inverted-L: about 60 mm up the box wall, 25 mm under the lid. Cut it long and trim for best RSSI.

Mount the radio and antenna as high in the box as possible. Also buy one 868 MHz FPC or helical antenna for an A/B test. Final antenna by week 8, after the antenna consultants review it.

Rules that constrain the radio

  • Unlicensed spectrum only, compliant with CNAF 2017 UN notes, IEEE C95.1 (RF exposure) and ESD safety. rule
  • No chip implementing a standard wireless access protocol (WiFi, Bluetooth, 2G–5G, ZigBee; the Session 1 slide adds NB-IoT and LoRa). rule
  • Nothing may stick out of the 15 × 15 × 7 cm IKEA 365+ box except buttons, switches and connectors, so the antennas go inside. The box sits on a support of about 70 cm. rule
  • Modules shown at Quick Mode are final. Antennas, batteries and mechanics may still change. rule
  • Distances: SRI 70 m, MRM ~260 m with every team transmitting at once, NM relays along the South and East rims with no line of sight end to end. The link must recover from "attacks". rule
  • Scoring counts only the first unbroken run of byte-identical lines (~10,000 lines of ~100 B), so reliability beats raw bit rate. rule

Antenna height is the biggest lever

At 0.75 m the ground reflection takes over beyond ~13 m (868) or ~37 m (2.4 GHz). Past that point loss grows 40 dB per decade and hardly depends on frequency: PR ≈ EIRP·GR·(h₁h₂)²/d⁴.

Raising both antennas from 0.5 to 1.0 m adds +12 dB. Mounting the antenna at the top of the box is free margin. Whether "about 70 cm" lets us use a taller support is a question for Atenea.

The 868 MHz antenna is longer than the box is tall

A 868 MHz quarter-wave is ~86 mm; the box is 70 mm tall. Our options are a helical or meander antenna (shorter, lower gain), a bent inverted-L, a PCB antenna, or a horizontal wire or dipole laid across the 21 cm diagonal.

A horizontal antenna gives up the all-round coverage NM needs. At 2.4 GHz a vertical λ/4 is 31 mm and fits easily. Even at −5 dBi per end, 868 keeps +30 dB at 70 m and +9 dB at 260 m.

Datasheet limits that matter

  • RFM69HCW: +20 dBm is limited to 1 % duty cycle (datasheet §3.3.7), so plan on +17 dBm for 2-minute rounds. verified
  • CC1101 at 250 kBaud needs a 540 kHz filter, wider than the 250 kHz sub-band. Its practical ceiling there is ~100 kbps.
  • nRF24L01+: 32 B maximum payload, 0 dBm. PA+LNA variants must be turned down to stay within 10 mW e.i.r.p.

Throughput: does the file fit in 2 minutes?

About 1 MB in 120 s needs 66.7 kbps of useful throughput, or 33.3 kbps with 2× compression. The model assumes selective-repeat ARQ with one ACK per 16 frames, 1 ms turnaround assumption, and 85 % efficiency for the micro and USB assumption. Bandwidth uses Carson's rule estimate.

Handoff to electronics

The radio team picks the transceiver and the electronics team adapts and picks the micro. This is what the board must provide. Part: HopeRF RFM69HCW, 868 MHz version (the 433 and 915 versions have different matching). The bare module is a small SMD part. For QM a ready-made breakout is easier, e.g. Adafruit "RFM69HCW 868/915 MHz" with regulator and level shifting. Package size and part number are to verify against the datasheet and distributor before ordering. Order 4 (2 boxes + 2 spares) from a distributor, not a marketplace.

Questions to ask

Atenea forum (posted by the project leader)

  • Allowed chips. Is a proprietary FSK transceiver like the RFM69HCW (SX1231H) allowed? It implements no standard protocol. Is a chip that can do a standard PHY we never use (e.g. Si4463, 802.15.4g) also acceptable?
  • Duty cycle and the schedule. 869.40–869.65 MHz allows 10 % of any hour (360 s) per transmitter, and a 2-min sending round uses ~115 s. How will SRI, MRM, NM rounds and rehearsals be scheduled so teams stay within it? Is it assessed per transmitter?
  • 2.4 GHz power. May a non-WiFi design with frequency hopping or listen-before-talk use the 100 mW of UN-85 a), or must it stay at 10 mW (UN-85 b)?
  • Support height. Is the support fixed at 70 cm or "about 70 cm"? May the antenna sit anywhere inside the box, e.g. under the lid?
  • Cooperation. May teams agree a common NM radio setup and an MRM frequency split, sharing settings but not code?

Consultants

  • Anna Umbert (spectrum regulation): can we rely on the 10 % duty cycle, or should we implement LBT+AFA (EN 300 220)? What e.r.p. evidence does she expect in the ANNEX?
  • Concepción Santos / Carles Puente (antennas): best compact 868 antenna for a vertically polarised, omnidirectional pattern in a 70 mm-tall plastic box. How does a hand or the foil near the box detune it? Can we measure gain at the lab?
  • Paradells / Perelló (protocols, with the protocols team): sharing one 250 kHz channel among N teams for MRM.
