Radio brief: band, module and link budget

Team Santiago, Andrian, MarcosVersion 0.4 · Fri 9 Oct 2026Next session Fri 16 Oct (week 4)

New in v0.4: all four teams agreed to run every mode at 2.4 GHz, so 868 MHz is dropped. The radio is now the Ebyte E01-ML01DP5 (Nordic nRF24L01+ with PA and LNA) at 250 kbps, with two patch antennas per box and our own channel in MRM. Do not order the Adafruit RFM69 or the Molex antennas; the new shopping list is below. Order by Fri 16 Oct.

Every figure carries a tag: rule from the competition rules PDF, verified against a primary source, assumption to be replaced by a measurement, and estimate.

Bottom line

  1. 2.4 GHz is the right band for this competition. In MRM all teams transmit at once rule. At 868 MHz four teams would share one 250 kHz channel (about a quarter of 67 kbps each). At 2.4 GHz each team gets its own channel, there is no duty-cycle limit, and NM has one common radio for everybody.
  2. The price is margin. The limit is 10 mW e.i.r.p. (CNAF UN-85 b) verified. With the E01 and two patches, at 0.75 m: SRI 70 m +27 dB, MRM 260 m +4.4 dB, NM 100 m hop +10.9 dB, against +36.7 / +13.9 / +20.5 dB at 868 MHz.
  3. MRM at 260 m is the thin link. Two levers: antennas 25 cm higher give +5 dB, and a "yes" to Atenea Q3 (100 mW for a frequency-hopping radio) gives +10 dB.
  4. Speed is no longer a problem. 250 kbps gives ~159 kbps of goodput. The simulator delivers the whole file in 28 s at 5 % loss, and all 10,000 lines even without compression (73 s).
  5. One omni instead of two patches costs 4 dB on every link and leaves MRM at +0.4 dB. It is simpler, so it stays an option, but only with taller supports or 100 mW. A middle option: one patch for SRI/MRM plus one omni for NM.
  6. MRM channel plan for the four teams: RF_CH 24 / 49 / 74 / 82. A neighbouring box 2 m away can arrive 55 dB above our 260 m signal, so the channels sit 25 MHz apart where possible and the two closest teams need 10 m between boxes.

No duty-cycle limit

UN-85 b) has no duty-cycle condition, so the 3-rounds-per-hour limit of 868 MHz is gone. Test as often as we like. The firmware's duty-cycle guard was removed.

The power limit is the only rule to watch: never above RF_PWR −18 dBm until T0 has measured the module.

Firmware ready before the hardware

A portable C driver for the nRF24L01+ passes 138 host checks against a simulated radio, including two radios talking over a simulated channel. It needs five board functions (SPI select and transfer, CE pin, microsecond delay and clock).

Frames use dynamic length and the radio's CRC, and go out without the chip's own ACKs, so our ARQ stays in charge. The protocol simulator runs on it: 11 of 11 scenarios pass.

Antennas

Final: two FR4 patches per box, on the front and back walls, each on its own small board with a pigtail to its own E01. Vertical polarisation. The front patch points at the peer in SRI and MRM; in NM the chain neighbours sit on both sides.

Quick Mode: a 2 dBi SMA whip on the module. Antennas may change after QM rule.

Two patches or one omni?

The E01 cannot go lower than about +7 dBm, so an antenna with little gain leaves part of the 10 dBm allowance unused, and the receive side loses the same gain. A single 2 dBi omni costs about 4 dB on every link compared with the patches. Model at 0.75 m:

Why a single omni is tempting

  • One radio per box, no patch boards, no VNA tuning
  • Off-the-shelf antenna with a known pattern
  • No aiming, and full coverage in NM

Why we still propose patches

  • MRM at 260 m has no margin left with the omni
  • A patch rejects a neighbouring team's box beside it by ~10 dB; an omni does not, so the closest pair of teams would need 15–20 m between boxes instead of 10 m
  • Middle option: front patch for SRI/MRM + omni for NM, one E01 each

Patch design notes

FR4 1.6 mm, 2.44 GHz: about 37.4 × 28.9 mm on a ~60 × 60 mm ground, inset-fed 50 Ω. It must stand vertically on a wall: flat on the carrier it would point at the sky. FR4's permittivity varies enough to move the resonance by ±55 MHz while the patch is only ~27 MHz wide, so we make a coupon with five lengths (28.0–30.0 mm) and measure it on the lab VNA inside the box assumption.

Rules that constrain the radio

  • Unlicensed spectrum only, compliant with CNAF 2017 UN notes, IEEE C95.1 (RF exposure) and ESD safety. rule
  • No chip implementing a standard wireless access protocol (WiFi, Bluetooth, 2G–5G, ZigBee; the Session 1 slide adds NB-IoT and LoRa). The nRF24L01+ has none of them. rule
  • Nothing may stick out of the 15 × 15 × 7 cm IKEA 365+ box except buttons, switches and connectors, so the antennas go inside. The box sits on a support of about 70 cm. rule
  • Modules shown at Quick Mode are final. Antennas, batteries and mechanics may still change. rule
  • Distances: SRI 70 m, teams one after another, 2 minutes; MRM ~260 m, all teams at the same time, 2 minutes; NM relays along the South and East rims with no line of sight end to end, 5 minutes. rule
  • Scoring counts only the first unbroken run of byte-identical lines (~10,000 lines of ~100 B), so reliability beats raw bit rate. rule

Spectrum: 2400–2483.5 MHz and our channels

nRF24 channel N sits at 2400 + N MHz, so only RF_CH 0–83 are legal in Spain; the driver refuses anything higher. Campus WiFi (eduroam) normally uses channels 1, 6 and 11, which leave three gaps. The MRM plan puts three teams in those gaps, 25 MHz apart, and the fourth at the band edge. Test T14 (campus survey) confirms it before we propose it.

MRM: one channel per team

Our ARQ makes both ends transmit (data one way, ACKs back), and the four teams' boxes stand close together at each end. Another team's box a few metres away is far louder than our partner 260 m away. Whether we still hear our partner depends on how far apart the channels are. The nRF24's selectivity at 250 kbps is −50 dB beyond 6 MHz and −60 dB beyond 25 MHz (datasheet, table 8) verified.

Antenna height is the biggest lever

At 0.75 m the ground reflection takes over beyond ~18 m at 2.4 GHz. Past that point loss grows 40 dB per decade and hardly depends on frequency: PR ≈ EIRP·GR·(h₁h₂)²/d⁴.

Raising both antennas from 0.75 to 1.0 m adds +5 dB on MRM. Mount the patches as high on the wall as the box allows. Whether "about 70 cm" allows a taller support is Atenea Q4.

The nRF24 has no RSSI

It only has one bit (RPD) that says "above −64 dBm at the chip", about −76 dBm at the E01's antenna port. Range tests therefore measure packet loss, and levels come from the spectrum analyser or the model. RPD still works as listen-before-talk and for the campus channel survey.

Datasheet limits that matter

  • 32 B maximum payload: our frames shrank from 64 B to 32 B (28 B of data). verified
  • Variable-length frames need the chip's auto-ACK feature on, so the driver sends every frame as "no ACK": no chip ACKs or retries on air.
  • PA always in line: no bypass, so the lowest output is ~+7 dBm.

Throughput: does the file fit in 2 minutes?

About 1 MB in 120 s needs 66.7 kbps of useful throughput. The model assumes selective-repeat ARQ with one ACK per 16 frames and 85 % efficiency for the micro and USB assumption. The simulator runs the real protocol code over a modelled channel with 5 % random loss, deflate compression, and the nRF24 airtime from the driver.

Questions to ask

Atenea forum (posted by the project leader)

  • Allowed chips. Is the nRF24L01+ on the Ebyte E01-ML01DP5 allowed? Its packet format is Nordic-proprietary and it has no Bluetooth.
  • 2.4 GHz power. May a radio that hops over ≥ 15 channels with listen-before-talk use the 100 mW of UN-85 a), or must every team stay at 10 mW (UN-85 b)? Worth +10 dB on MRM.
  • Support height and box. Is the support fixed at 70 cm? May the box stand on its 15 × 7 cm edge?
  • Cooperation. May teams agree a common NM radio and an MRM channel split, sharing settings but not code?
  • Two antennas. Please confirm a device may use two antennas inside the box.
